# Product coherence panel — 2026-07-26

Five people evaluated Assoluto independently, each grounded in BOTH the live site
(https://assoluto.eu, GET-only) and the code at `b94348a`. A judge then synthesised.
All 6 agents completed; the load-bearing claims were re-verified by hand afterwards
(see the verification notes in [README.md](README.md)).

## The core tension

Assoluto is an upmarket product wearing a downmarket price tag, and it has papered over the gap with claims the code does not support. The engineering targets a 30-150 person contract manufacturer — consignment stock with signed movements, per-customer catalog pricing, an on-time SLA heatmap, a tenant-wide audit log — while the pricing page addresses a "malá dílna, 3 uživatelé" at 490 CZK and the homepage testimonials cite shops of 8, 12 and 22 employees. That mismatch is why the marketing has drifted from the code: to make a system built for a serious manufacturer sound worth 490 CZK to a workshop, the copy had to promise the outcomes (revision control, one-click stocktake, set-the-deadline, daily backups of drawings, take-your-data-and-leave) rather than the mechanisms — and the outcomes are precisely what is unbuilt. The founder's instinct to reach for a lower-friction buyer is understandable, but at 490 CZK no acquisition channel a solo founder can run is arithmetically viable, no one runs a vendor review so the compliance work is wasted, and the free unlimited Community tier sits at the top of the pricing page absorbing every technically capable prospect. Repositioning is the change that makes the rest coherent: the same code sold to 20-150 employee subcontract manufacturers at 3,000-8,000 CZK/mo, priced on active client companies rather than the customer's own headcount, gives the product a buyer with a budget line, gives the CTO's due-diligence list a reason to exist, and gives the founder an ACV that can support twenty founder-led calls a week. But repositioning upmarket only survives contact with that buyer if the claim surface is true first — because the bigger the customer, the more thoroughly someone checks.

## Coherence verdict

There is one story, and all five personas found the same crack in it from five different angles: the site describes a product that the code has not finished building, and the gap is widest exactly where each persona's trust is decided. Petr checks the exit and finds a ZIP export promised in four places including the binding Terms — `grep -rniE "zipfile|ZipFile" app/ scripts/` returns nothing. Jana checks the deadline and finds `promised_delivery_at` read by the PDF, the CSV, the detail template and the entire SLA dashboard, with zero writes anywhere in the repo, while /features sells "Oceníte a stanovíte termín". The CTO checks the backup and finds `scripts/backup.sh:74` rclones the pg_dump directory only, while the homepage tells buyers their drawings are backed up daily. The investor checks the wedge and finds no `revision`, `supersedes_id` or `is_current` column on `order_attachments`, under a headline that promises "the right revision next to the right order — every time". Four independent reviewers, four different pages, one failure mode. That is not a copy problem; it is a discipline problem that has now reached the contract. The second coherence break is structural: this is a two-sided product where each side is one layer short. Jana cannot set a date, edit an order, or duplicate one; Martina presses Confirmed on a quote and `orders.py:1247-1265` routes the notification through `_contact_recipients`, so the supplier is never told. The supplier side is a filing cabinet and the client side is a dead end, and the two failures multiply — the portal's whole ROI is clients self-serving, and nothing in the product or the platform dashboard measures whether a single client ever logs in. Third: the money is not connected at either end. Stripe price IDs have been NULL across three consecutive audits, nurture emails ship behind `trial_nurture_enabled=False`, and the MRR tile counts `trialing` and `demo` as revenue, so the founder's own dashboard will show him money nobody is paying while checkout no-ops. The product is genuinely good — RLS isolation, a real 9-state machine, Argon2id, single-use tokens, an audited support-access grant, trilingual legal writing better than most enterprise vendors. None of that is in question. What is in question is that a superbly engineered system is pointed at an 8-person dílna paying 490 CZK, with a feature set (consignment stock, per-customer price lists, on-time SLA reporting, full audit trail) that describes a 30-150 person subcontract manufacturer, and with a claim surface written for a customer who will never read it as carefully as this panel just did. The verdict is decisive: stop building, spend one week making every public claim true, then spend three weeks finishing the daily loop on both sides — and while that runs, make ten discovery calls into the segment the product was actually built for.

## Consensus problems

### [blocker] The site and the binding Terms promise a full data export (orders + customers CSV, attachments as ZIP) that does not exist in the code

*Raised independently by: prospect, cto-dpo, staff-user*

Four public surfaces make the promise — index.html:430, the JSON-LD copy at :533, pricing.html:168, privacy.html:144 — and terms.html:123 escalates it to a contractual statement: "The CSV / ZIP export endpoints are available at any time during the active subscription." I verified independently: `grep -rniE "zipfile|ZipFile" app/ scripts/` returns zero hits. The only bulk export in the product is `@router.get(".csv")` at app/routers/orders.py:228, which emits order headers only — no items, comments, status history or attachments. There is no customers CSV, no products CSV, no assets CSV. `/app/me/profile/export` is a per-person GDPR JSON blob, not business data. Three personas hit this from different doors: Petr reads the exit answer first because a Czech SaaS folded on him once; the CTO writes it into the risk register and fails the vendor on it; Jana discovers the assets side of it at stocktake time. It is simultaneously the trust story, the compliance blocker, and — because the promise sits in §8 of the Terms — a misrepresentation, not merely stale copy.

### [blocker] Nobody can set a promised delivery date, so the SLA dashboard is permanently zero and the feature page sells a capability that has no input anywhere

*Raised independently by: staff-user, client-contact*

`Order.promised_delivery_at` is declared at app/models/order.py:83 and consumed everywhere — orders.py:342 (CSV), pdf_service.py:227, orders/detail.html:110, and every query in sla_service.py:55-173 filters on `promised_delivery_at IS NOT NULL`. I confirmed `grep -rn promised_delivery app/ scripts/` shows reads only: no Form field, no service parameter, no route writes it; `create_order` (order_service.py:314) accepts `requested_delivery_at` alone. So the tile renders "—" forever and `/app/admin/sla` shows 0 orders and a 0% on-time rate for every tenant that will ever exist, while /features sells "Oceníte a stanovíte termín". Jana calls it the most important field of her day; Martina independently identifies its absence from the QUOTED email as the reason she cannot act on a notification without logging in. This is one missing form field with an outsized blast radius: it disables an entire advertised dashboard and undermines the client-side value proposition at the same time.

### [blocker] A contact confirming or cancelling a quote notifies nobody at the supplier

*Raised independently by: client-contact*

Raised by one persona but verified line-by-line and it is the single sharpest defect in the codebase. app/routers/orders.py:1247-1265 branches on `if target == OrderStatus.SUBMITTED` → staff payload, `else` → `build_order_status_changed`, and that builder resolves recipients solely via `_contact_recipients` (notification_service.py:160-163). Contacts are permitted QUOTED→CONFIRMED and →CANCELLED (order_service.py:103-105). Therefore the most commercially loaded click in the system emails the actor's own colleagues, including the actor (no `exclude_email` is passed, unlike `build_order_comment`), and sends nothing to the shop. Same hole in the bulk path at orders.py:1140-1156. `grep 'status_changed' tests/` covers only audit feeds, so no test would catch it. Consequence: a confirmed order sits untouched until the client phones to ask why, and a cancelled order keeps being manufactured. One occurrence of either ends the account — and it silently invalidates the product's entire premise. The fix is small: branch on `principal.is_staff` rather than on target status, build both payloads, exclude the actor.

### [blocker] The attachment bucket — the customers' CAD files — is not backed up at any interval, while the homepage says drawings are backed up daily

*Raised independently by: cto-dpo*

Verified: scripts/backup.sh does `pg_dump`, gzip, optional GPG, and `rclone sync "${BACKUP_DIR}" "${RCLONE_REMOTE}/pg"` at line 74 — the bucket is never touched. docs/BACKUP_RESTORE.md:12-15 states this explicitly, and docs/PRELAUNCH_REVIEW_2026-04-25.md:215-217 already flagged it as "unrecoverable" months ago and it remains open. Meanwhile index.html:424 and the JSON-LD at :503 market "drawings and attachments are stored in EU-based cloud storage. Backups run daily and are kept for 14 days." The failure mode is silent: the database survives a bucket loss and renders attachment rows pointing at objects that are gone, so nobody learns until a download 404s. Compounded with the missing bulk export, the customer's IP exists in exactly one unbacked-up bucket that cannot be pulled down in bulk — which is what ended the CTO's review. `rclone sync` of the bucket plus object versioning is a fifteen-line change to a script that already imports rclone.

### [blocker] The paid funnel is disconnected at both ends and the founder's own MRR number hides it

*Raised independently by: prospect, founder-strategy*

`stripe_price_starter`/`stripe_price_pro` default to "" (config.py:129-130), migration 1003 seeds `platform_plans.stripe_price_id` NULL, `_sync_stripe_prices_from_env` only UPSERTs a non-empty value, and docs/audit-runs/2026-07-03-1507 F-BE-002 records them still unset in production across three consecutive audit runs — so paid checkout silently no-ops. `trial_nurture_enabled` defaults False (config.py:86), so trial_day1/day7/ending templates never send. TRIAL_DAYS = 30 with no card required (and the comment above service.py:583 says "14-day trial", so the code disagrees with itself about the length). And the MRR query I verified at platform_admin.py:232-244 filters `Subscription.status.in_(("active","trialing","demo"))` while every signup auto-creates a trialing Starter subscription (service.py:583) — so ten free signups display 4,900 CZK of "MRR" that nobody is paying, on a system where the pay button does nothing. Petr reaches it from the buyer's side ("this is the one moment where you either get 490 Kč from me or lose me"); the investor reaches it from the metrics side. Pre-revenue is currently not evidence of anything, because the test has never been run.

### [major] The invitation email reads as phishing and every subsequent notification is content-free, so the client side never activates

*Raised independently by: client-contact, staff-user*

The entire invitation body is four lines with no benefit statement, no sender identity, and no human sentence (app/email/templates/invitation.txt); the invite form takes only `email` and `full_name` (customers.py:276-345). Every message ships from one global address — sender.py:147 sets `msg["From"] = self._settings.smtp_from` — with no `Reply-To` and no per-tenant sender field, so the envelope says Assoluto while the signature says 4MEX, and any reply lands in the founder's inbox rather than the supplier's. Then `order_status_changed.txt` carries only number, status label, title and URL for all eight statuses, even though `quoted_total` and `promised_delivery_at` are on the row and already rendered on screen — so each email is a demand to log in. No `List-Unsubscribe` header, no digest, and `notification_prefs` exists on both User and CustomerContact but I confirmed it is read nowhere outside gdpr_service.py. Jana names the consequence for the supplier (client replies vanish into Outlook and the comment thread is half a conversation); Martina names it for herself (a Gmail filter is her only defence, which also kills the QUOTED email). This is the mechanism by which the supplier's ROI fails to materialise and he churns in month two.

### [major] Every order is write-once and typed from scratch — no edit route, no duplicate, no due-date column

*Raised independently by: staff-user*

Verified against the router: app/routers/orders.py exposes create, item add/patch/delete, transition, comment, bulk transition, PDF and CSV — there is no `GET /{order_id}/edit` and no `POST /{order_id}`, while customers.py and products.py both have edit routes. So `title`, `notes`, `requested_delivery_at` and `customer_id` are immutable after creation, and Jana's only recourse for "move it to the 15th" is a comment nobody reads or a cancel-and-retype that burns a number and fires a cancellation email at the client. `grep -rniE "duplicate|clone|reorder"` across routers/services/templates finds only `DuplicateProductSku`. Adding a line item is a plain POST-redirect, one full page load per line. The orders list accepts only status/customer/q/page, has no delivery-date column and hardcodes `order_by(Order.created_at.desc())` — even though `build_orders_query` already supports date_from/date_to and the CSV route exposes them, so no UI can produce the params. At sixty repeat orders a month this is more work than the whiteboard it replaces, which is why Jana would run the trial and not switch.

### [major] The headline wedge — drawing revision control — has no schema behind it

*Raised independently by: founder-strategy*

index.html:145 promises "The right revision next to the right order — every time", features.html:16 sells "drawings, revisions, deadlines", and index.html:94 makes wrong-revision parts the headline pain. I confirmed app/models/attachment.py:24-67 carries kind, filename, content_type, size_bytes, storage_key, thumbnail_key and uploader — no `revision`, no `version`, no `supersedes_id`, no `is_current` — and attachment_service.py does no filename-family grouping. Upload rev-A on Monday and rev-B on Thursday and you get two files in a flat list with nothing marking A obsolete: the exact Outlook failure the homepage claims to eliminate. This is the most defensible differentiator in the product (no ERP B2B module does it well, and a production manager will pay to never get it wrong again) and it is currently the least true sentence on the site. Roughly a week of work: two columns, normalised-stem grouping on upload, a collapsed/struck-through render, and an audit event.

### [major] Neither the supplier nor the founder can see whether any client ever logs in

*Raised independently by: founder-strategy, client-contact*

`CustomerContact` has `invited_at` and `accepted_at` and no `last_login_at`, while staff `User` does have one (user.py:45). customers/detail.html:115-121 renders three states — disabled / active / invited — where "active" only means an invite was accepted once, possibly in March. No router or service counts contact logins, and the platform dashboard counts signups, subscriptions and MRR but no activation metric. `TokenPurpose.MAGIC_LINK` is declared at tokens.py:38 with no implementation (confirmed: `grep -rni magic app/` returns the enum, the module docstring and a comment), and `link_contact_to_identity` at platform/service.py:147 — a fully written function that would give a contact one login across all their Assoluto suppliers — has exactly one hit in the entire repo, its own definition. Client adoption is the single variable that determines whether the portal delivers value or is quietly abandoned, and it is the one thing nobody in the system can observe.

*Raised independently by: cto-dpo*

The escape hatch itself verifies: AGPL-3.0, public repo, and the CTO confirmed `git ls-remote` shows main at the deployed commit — the published source really is the running software, which is a better continuity answer than the source-code escrow an enterprise reviewer would normally demand. ## What multiple personas independently praised

- Tenant isolation is enforced in Postgres RLS, not in application code — the portal/portal_app two-role split, `set_config('app.tenant_id', tid, true)` in deps.py, migration 0003_enable_rls. Praised independently by the prospect, the CTO and the investor, and it is one of the few homepage claims ("vynuceno přímo v databázi, ne jen v aplikaci") the code fully backs.
- The AGPL escape hatch is genuine rather than decorative: public repo, deployed commit matches published main, and FEATURE_PLATFORM=true ships the whole SaaS layer including billing rather than a hollowed-out core. The prospect calls it a real answer to "what if you quit" (he has been burned before), the CTO rates it above the source-code escrow he would normally demand.
- The legal and commercial paperwork is more mature than most funded startups manage — a correct Art. 28 controller/processor split stated identically in CS/EN/DE, per-category legal bases, a per-category retention schedule citing 563/1991 Sb., a 72-hour breach commitment, 30-day subprocessor-change notice, and honest §8 language that Starter and Pro carry no SLA. /cookies was singled out as the most honest cookie page the CTO read this year.
- The order state machine is production-grade: a real 9-state pipeline, status history, per-transition audit events, internal-vs-external comments, `_backfill_milestones` that never overwrites an existing timestamp, and the documented BackgroundTasks/explicit-commit discipline. The operator and the investor praised it independently; 494 tests back it.
- The CSV export was written by someone who has watched a Czech back office open a file — 500-row streaming batches, UTF-8 BOM, `;` delimiter, CRLF, plain-dot decimals. It opens correctly in CZ Excel first try, which almost nothing does.
- GDPR erasure is real anonymisation rather than a soft-delete flag: every PII field nulled or masked, password_hash cleared, session_version bumped to kill live sessions, email rewritten to a unique erased-user placeholder so the tenant-scoped UNIQUE survives re-signup, row id preserved for referential integrity. The CTO notes most vendors get this wrong in one direction or the other.
- The platform-admin support-access flow is designed exactly as a reviewer would ask: no implicit read into tenant data, an explicit grant creating a real User plus a TenantMembership with access_type='support', and a `platform.support_access_granted` event written into the target tenant's own audit log, with a symmetric revoke.
- Customer permissions default to permissive and are enforced server-side, not merely hidden in the template — `OrderPermissions.from_dict` returns all-True on an empty dict and `can_upload_files` is re-checked in the handler. The client contact specifically contrasts this with portals that default her to read-only nothing.
- The ⌘K command palette (arrow navigation, focus trap, focus restore, staff-side customer-name matching) and the HTMX inline item editing with per-field autosave inside a SAVEPOINT are the two places the app feels built for someone fast.
- The Czech is written by someone who has stood in a workshop — "Furt hledám v Outlooku, kterou verzi výkresu klient poslal minulý týden" — with CZK prices, a real imprint, and the Excel/Assoluto/big-ERP comparison table that the prospect called the best thing on the site. The honest "we don't have Pohoda yet, it's on the roadmap" answer buys more trust than a claim would.
- The assets ledger — the client's own tooling and material held at the supplier, with signed movements and a live balance — is the one capability the client contact says email genuinely cannot replace, and it is currently undersold on every public page.

## Genuine conflicts between personas (and the chair's call)

**Tension.** Jana (daily operator) needs bulk status transitions to move twenty orders in one gesture; Martina (client contact) needs to never receive a wrong 'Delivered' email, and today one mis-picked dropdown sends twenty irreversible ones with no confirmation and no undo. Slowing Jana down protects Martina; protecting Martina taxes the operator's fastest tool.

**Call.** Martina wins on the send, Jana wins on the click. Keep bulk transition exactly as fast, add a `data-confirm` naming the count and the target status, and — the real fix — decouple the mutation from the notification: a per-transition 'notify the client' checkbox defaulted on for forward moves and off for backward moves and DRAFT, plus outright suppression of customer email for moves into DRAFT. Jana loses one keystroke; Martina loses the class of email that would make her filter the whole domain. An email is irreversible and a confirmation dialog is not, so when in doubt the irreversible side wins.

**Tension.** Petr (prospect) is a small CZ shop for whom 490 CZK is already a considered purchase and who values the visible free self-hosted tier as proof his data is not hostage; the investor says the ACV cannot support any channel a solo founder can run, that Community should move off the pricing page, and that the price should be 3,000-8,000 CZK aimed at 20-150 employee manufacturers.

**Call.** The investor wins on segment and price; Petr wins on voice and on the escape hatch. The feature set decides it — consignment stock with signed movements, per-customer catalog pricing and an on-time SLA heatmap are not bought by an 8-person dílna, and at 490 CZK the arithmetic forbids outbound, paid acquisition and the compliance work a serious buyer demands. But do not delete the trust position that made Petr's report warm: keep AGPL and self-hosting as a prominently linked proof on /self-hosted, keep the Excel/ERP comparison table, keep the workshop Czech. Move Community to the bottom of the pricing page, stop leading the hero with the licence, and sell the hosted tier on operations (tested restores, monitoring, onboarding) rather than on bits. Same product, same voice, a buyer with a budget line.

## Skeptical CTO + DPO doing vendor due diligence (`cto-dpo`)

**Verdict.** The paperwork is far better than I expected from a solo-founder product and the engineering has real security judgement in it — Postgres RLS for tenant isolation, Argon2id, a strict CSP, HSTS with includeSubDomains, correct CSRF double-submit, single-use recovery tokens, and a support-access flow that refuses to let the operator read tenant data without an audited grant. I would not have predicted that from the price point. But the review does not turn on the parts that are good, it turns on the parts I can check and cannot get past. Three of those are hard stops. The subprocessor table in the privacy policy omits the provider that the vendor's own deployment docs say holds every uploaded drawing and the entire nightly database dump; I can demonstrate the disclosure is incomplete from public repository text, which means my supplier would be signing a DPA with a provably wrong Annex. The CSV-and-ZIP export that the binding terms, the pricing page and the homepage FAQ all promise as our exit path returns zero hits for ZIP anywhere in the codebase — the only bulk export that exists is a CSV of order headers. And the attachment bucket is not backed up at all, which the founder already knows and wrote down in his own pre-launch review as "unrecoverable", while the homepage tells buyers their drawings are backed up daily. Those three compound into one sentence I have to write in my report: our CAD files would exist in exactly one unbacked-up bucket that we cannot pull down in bulk, held by a processor nobody disclosed. Add no MFA and no SSO — `totp_secret` is a declared column that nothing writes, and the password floor is eight characters — and I have failed the vendor on identity grounds before I even reach data protection. The single-VPS architecture with Postgres on a local volume is survivable for a supplier of this size and the terms are honest that Starter and Pro carry no SLA, but "Hetzner runs redundant infrastructure" on the homepage is misdirection about a box with no replica and no failover, and naming UptimeRobot on the pricing page when monitoring is a best-effort GitHub Actions cron is a checkable false statement. What frustrates me is how close this is: the gap is mostly between what the site claims and what the code does, and closing it is days of work, not quarters.

**Decision.** I would fail this vendor review and block our purchasing team from putting drawings, prices or order volumes into the supplier's Assoluto portal — not on the architecture, which is defensible for a supplier this size, but because the exit path promised in the contract does not exist in the code and the drawings are not backed up, so a single bucket loss destroys our IP with no recourse beyond a €300 liability cap against a sole trader. I would send the supplier a five-item remediation list (subprocessor list corrected, full-account ZIP export shipped, attachment bucket backed up, TOTP enabled, DPA published) and offer to re-review in 90 days; in the meantime we would exchange drawings the old way. If the vendor fixed the export and the attachment backup, the identity gaps alone would move this from a block to a conditional approval with compensating controls.

## Operator-investor: business model & strategy (`founder-strategy`)

**Verdict.** This is an excellent product aimed at a customer who cannot pay for it, sold through a channel that does not exist, with a wedge that is marketed but not built. The engineering is not the problem — RLS-enforced isolation, a real 9-state order machine, 494 tests, GDPR export, invoice PDFs, three locales — this is a better-built system than most Series A products I have seen. The problem is that all of that quality is pointed at a 8-person dílna paying 490 CZK, while the feature set (consignment stock with signed movements, per-customer price lists, on-time SLA reporting, a full audit trail) describes a 30-150 person contract manufacturer that would happily pay 5,000. Three facts compound into a dead end: the one thing the homepage promises hardest — \"the right revision next to the right order, every time\" — has no revision column in `order_attachments`; nothing in the product or in your own platform dashboard tells anyone whether the supplier's clients ever log in, which is the only variable that decides whether the portal delivers value or is quietly abandoned; and the paid funnel is not connected at either end (Stripe price IDs still NULL after three audits, nurture emails shipped behind a false flag, 30-day no-card trial). Pre-revenue is therefore not evidence of anything yet — you have never actually run the test. Two things you got right and should not second-guess: the EU/open-source/RLS trust position is genuinely differentiated in Czechia, and the commercial paperwork (IČO capture, CZ invoice by transfer, refund and exit policy) is more mature than most funded startups manage. Point the same product at a customer with a budget, build revision chains, instrument client logins, and this is a real business. Leave it as positioned and it is a superb portfolio piece that will accumulate five hobbyist self-hosters and no revenue.

**Decision.** As an operator-investor I would not back this at its current positioning — the ACV cannot support any acquisition channel a solo founder can run, and the headline feature is unbuilt. I would fund the founder to run 20 paid pilots at 3,000-5,000 CZK/mo against 20-150 employee subcontract manufacturers, on the condition that revision chains, client-login visibility and a working Stripe checkout ship before the first call — and I would revisit within 90 days on one number: how many pilot tenants have three or more client contacts logging in weekly.

**What works**

Tenant isolation is enforced in Postgres RLS (migrations/versions/0003_enable_rls.py, the portal/portal_app two-role split documented in CLAUDE.md §1, `set_config('app.tenant_id', ...)` in app/deps.py) — the homepage claim "vynuceno přímo v databázi, ne jen v aplikaci" is one of the few claims on the site that the code fully backs. - The core order workflow is complete, coherent and not a demo. `app/services/order_service.py` is 833 lines with a real 9-state machine, status history, per-transition audit events, internal-vs-external comments, and the BackgroundTasks/explicit-commit discipline documented in CLAUDE.md §2. Notification recipient resolution (`app/services/notification_service.py:66-116`) correctly splits staff and contact audiences. 494 tests. This is production-grade work.
- The commercial paperwork is unusually mature for a pre-revenue product.
